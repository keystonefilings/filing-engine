How a filing runs

One pass from intake to filed documents.

State sites keep no drafts, so every filing is a single run from the first screen to the last. Everything is checked before it starts.

Intake

Your system or your customer's sends the entity details. Your onboarding answers fill the rest: agent, organizer, signer, addresses.

Approval

The client receives a link with every value and the fees. Approving binds those exact values to the run.

Pre-flight

Site hours, logins, account balance and fee table are checked before a browser opens.

Run

The engine reads each screen live, fills it by label and keeps a screenshot and page copy of every step.

EIN in the same pass

After the name check passes, the EIN is filed and its letter saved, then entered into the state form.

Filed

Documents are stored, and signed webhooks report each stage to your systems.

Controls

Built for filings that cannot be taken back.

A government submission is final. Every control below exists to stop a wrong filing before it happens, and to prove what happened after.

Halts when a state changes a form

Each screen is compared with its last approved structure: fields, required marks, options and prices. Any difference stops the run before submission. Your reviewer sees the change and a proposed mapping, and accepts or corrects it.

Checks the price before paying

Fees are quoted at approval from a verified fee table and read again on the payment screen. NJ charges $50 for an LLC standing certificate and $25 for a corporation's, so the engine also confirms the entity type on the state record.

Never submits twice

A record is written before the final click. If a machine stops mid-run, that filing is flagged for reconciliation and never re-run on its own. Nothing else ever runs over a filing that may already be at the state.

Shows where every value came from

Each filled field records its source: the filing itself, your intake, the customer's onboarding answers or your company defaults. Every screen of every run keeps a screenshot and a copy of the page.

For service companies

Your company, your accounts, your customers' rules.

Each subscriber runs independently. Your customers (CPAs, title companies, payroll companies) sign up under your brand and answer the questions you choose to ask them.

  • Your state logins. Stored under your account only, never shared with another subscriber.
  • Your services on the forms. Your registered agent service, organizer, signer and filer.
  • Defaults and locks. Set the answer for any customer question, or lock it for all of them.
  • Your brand. Approval pages carry your name and your support contact.
  • Your own fixed address. Government sites refuse many cloud addresses, so your filings run from a machine on your own IP.
Your company
Accounts and services. NJ and ny.gov logins, NY drawdown account, registered agent service, people who sign and file, defaults for your customers.
Your customer
Their rules. Who is the registered agent, who organizes and signs, who approves, which state accounts their filings use: yours, or their own.
The entity
The facts. Name, owners, addresses and the responsible party, from your intake.
Each filing
Final say. A value sent with a filing always overrides a default. A missing answer stops the filing and names the question.

Security

Sensitive data stays where it belongs.

Masked SSNs

Social Security numbers are masked on approval pages, in API responses and in recorded screens.

Secrets in a vault

State logins live in a vault under your account, never in settings, chat or email, and are read only when a run starts.

Single-use approval links

Signed, expiring links, bound to the exact data shown. An optional one-time code adds a second check.

Signed webhooks

Every event carries a timestamped signature, with retries and a dead-letter record.

Dry-run mode

New sites and new customers can run every screen and stop before submission until you switch them live.

Separate subscribers

Logins, jobs, customers and documents are kept per company. One subscriber's machine only runs its own filings.
