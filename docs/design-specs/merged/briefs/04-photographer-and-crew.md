# Brief: photographer and crew

**For:** one photographer and their production team (producer, casting director, location scout,
prop stylist, digital tech, retoucher, and the people the work needs around them).
**What:** every still photograph for the new cofile.app website, over about seven shoot days.
**Status:** for quotation. **Contact:** [CoFile contact].

A separate brief covers the 12-second film of the engraver at the bench. It shares the first two
shoot days with this brief, and we would like your production to make it if you shoot motion.

---

## 1. Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service for accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms.
In the site's own words: "CoFile files your clients' formations, EINs and payroll registrations on
the official state and IRS websites, once you approve." Firms use it under their own brand, so their
clients see only the firm: "Your name on the door. Ours in small type."

## 2. The look

The idea behind the site is that CoFile is the firm's stationer. A good stationer engraves the
firm's name on a plate, pulls a proof for the firm to approve before anything runs, and keeps its
own name off the firm's stationery. So the photographs are of a stationer's real objects and real
working people: two office doors with hand-engraved name plates, the same plate blank, a printer's
proof on a make-ready table, a desk where the papers come home, an empty stage under a ghost light,
a baker texting at dawn, a firm owner reading at her desk, and CoFile's own filing professionals at
work. The reference is a contemporary fine stationer and a well-made annual report.

**One light.** Every frame is lit from camera left: low sun or a north window at the left of the
frame, with practicals where the place has them. Natural light and practicals only, shaped with
flags and bounce. No strobes. Expose to hold the highlights in the brass.

**One grade** for the whole set, the film and the retouching: neutral and slightly warm, whites at
the site's paper white, true blacks, skin exactly as it is, checked against the person on set. No
teal and orange, no haze, no vignetting, no added grain, no black and white, no duotones.

**One metal.** The brass in the photographs is the brand's brass. No other gold, copper or yellow
appears in any picture.

**People** are mid-task, not posing, and don't look at the lens except in the tight team portraits.
Hands matter: a thumb on a phone, a hand near a trackpad, a burin.

**What the pictures are for.** Most photographs are a setting. On the site, live pages of the
product lie on top of them, so each frame keeps quiet areas where those pages go. The plates are
shot square-on so that pages, light and, on the blank plate, a visitor's own typed firm name can be
laid into them in exact register. Every frame is shot for its crops: each placement in section 3
has its own crop, and you deliver each crop as its own file, phone crops included, framed on the
day. A phone never gets a desktop frame squeezed to fit.

The site's first screen has no photograph (the owner's decision). The first photographs a visitor
sees are the two doors.

Red only ever means "something went wrong" on the site, so no red in frame (exit signs out of frame
or retouched dark). Never red, white and blue together.

**Never in frame:**

- Stock of any kind, AI-generated people or images, models standing in for CoFile's staff, or
  models in borrowed aprons standing in for people who bake.
- Anything official: seals, stamps, rosettes, ribbons, "approved" marks, flags, eagles, state
  outlines, capitol domes, columns, courthouses, certificate frames, parchment, or any government
  header, form or letter. No real state or IRS papers: papers are edge-on or face-down.
- Legible real business names or marks. Signage is removed, out of frame or soft. Logos on devices
  and clothing are taped on the day and retouched.
- The tired office: handshakes, high-fives, people pointing at laptops, smiling headsets, crowds
  round a screen, glass-walled startup offices, a stressed person rubbing their temples, piles of
  paperwork as a joke, anyone jumping for joy.
- Any messaging app's look on a screen: no app's bubbles, read receipts, typing dots or logos.
- Invented screens. Screens are shot dark or with tracking marks and replaced with captures of the
  real product, which we supply. If a screen can't be replaced cleanly, it faces away.
- Treatments: tilt, cut-outs, borders, frames, color washes, gradients, darkened corners.

**The locations** (the scout finds them; no names or numbers may show in frame):

- **Door A, Harbor & Pine CPA (C1, C3, C4).** A brick office building on a quiet street in Jersey
  City, Hoboken, Montclair or Brooklyn: a converted rowhouse or a small 1920s commercial building
  with a stone step and a transom over the door. A painted wooden door in a deep harbor-blue black,
  close to Harbor `#1D4E63` in shadow. The scout looks for one already that color; otherwise the set
  painter repaints it with the owner's permission and restores it after. A plain brass lever or
  knob. The plate is mounted on the brick to the right of the door, its centre 1,500 mm above the
  step. No house numbers, no names on mailboxes, no neighbouring signs, no intercom brands in frame.
  It must get late-afternoon sun from camera left raking at 20° to 30° across the wall, and the
  transom must be lit from inside at dusk, so we need access to the room behind it.
- **Door B, Northgate Payroll (C2).** A converted mill or a small modern office building: concrete
  or pale brick, the door's aluminium frame clear-anodized. Through the glass, a softly lit open
  office out of focus: no legible screens, no people in focus, no logos. The plate's centre at
  1,500 mm, to the right of the door, as at door A. Mid-morning light from camera left.
- **The north-window room (C6).** A studio or office with a large north window, and one oiled
  walnut desktop about 1.8 × 1.0 m, lightly figured, with no scratches that read as dirt.
- **The bakery (C5).** A real independent neighbourhood bakery in Jersey City, Union City or West
  New York, from 5:30 to 7:00 a.m.: ovens on, the shop still dark but for the work lights and the
  first daylight. A steel counter with flour on it, a tray just out of the oven, proofing loaves on
  a rack. Its signage out of frame or soft. Ideally the sisters' own bakery.
- **The CPA's office (C8).** Her own office, rearranged only for light: framed photographs, a
  plant, binders, a window, a legal pad. No government certificates or seals in frame; a framed
  licence on the wall is out of frame or soft past reading.
- **The letterpress shop (C9).** The make-ready table of a real letterpress or engraving shop, worn
  wood or a light table, the press soft in the background. This is the shop printing the props,
  which we are briefing separately; we arrange access, and the scout checks its light.
- **The theatre (C7).** A real small theatre or school auditorium in Jersey City, Newark, Queens or
  Miami: a proscenium, the curtain open, bare boards, rows of empty seats in the foreground falling
  to dark. No colored curtain or seats that would add a hue; no flags or emblems anywhere in the
  house. Exit signs out of frame or retouched dark.
- **CoFile's workplace (C10).** The team's real workplace, an office or a home office, at a time
  that suits them (question 12).
- **The engraver's bench (C11 stills).** The engraver's own workshop, shared with the film crew.
