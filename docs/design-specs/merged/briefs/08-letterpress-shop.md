# Brief: letterpress shop

**The job:** printed props for our photographs: business-card and letterhead proofs for a fictional
CPA firm, Harbor & Pine CPA, and a heavy white envelope addressed to it; and your make-ready table
as the location for one photograph.
**For:** a working letterpress or engraving shop that prints fine stationery on cotton stock.
**Contact:** [CoFile contact]

## 1. Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service. Accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms use it
to file state business filings for their clients (LLC formations, payroll registrations, EINs)
under the firm's own brand. We see ourselves as the firm's stationer, and one page of our site says
it in the printer's own terms: "You've approved a proof before: business cards, letterhead, an
invitation at the printer. Nothing went to press until you said so."

**About the name on the props.** Harbor & Pine CPA is a fictional example firm. It is not our client
and not a real business; we use it in every picture to show what CoFile does for a firm.

## 2. What we need

Real printed pieces for two photographs. One shows a printer's proof on a make-ready table: a sheet
of Harbor & Pine business cards and a Harbor & Pine letterhead proof, printed on cotton stock in
Harbor blue, with a pencil "OK" in the margin. The other is a top-down still life on a walnut desk:
a heavy white envelope addressed "Harbor & Pine CPA", the moment the firm's papers come home. We
supply the artwork; you match the inks, print and deliver the pieces before our shoot. We'd also
like to take the proof photograph in your shop, on your own make-ready table, so the setting is as
real as the printing.

### 3.1 The letterhead, as it must look

- **The logo:** a square symbol, a pine standing above two short wave lines, drawn in one weight,
  in Harbor blue. Print it exactly as supplied: never redrawn, recolored, outlined or stretched.
- **The typeset name:** "Harbor & Pine CPA" in Lora 600, the ampersand in Lora's italic, "CPA" in
  small capitals (or at 88% size with slightly opened spacing, as our files set it).
- **The Firm Rule:** a thick line in Harbor, a gap, then a thin line in Pine, in the proportion
  3 : 2 : 1 (thick line, gap, thin line). Our files set the exact weights.

### 3.2 Inks and color

| Ink | Hex (screen) | Used for |

| Pine | `#2B5A47` | The Firm Rule's thin line. The firm's second color |
| Plate Ink | `#1D2130` | The typeset name, as on screen (three-ink version); the envelope's address |

- Impression depth: your recommendation. The proof photograph is lit by raking window light from

### 3.3 The location

The proof photograph needs the make-ready table of a real letterpress or engraving shop: worn wood
or a light table, the press soft in the background, and raking window light from the camera's left.
Our location scout visits first to check the light. No people, or just the printer's hand and apron
at the edge of the frame. It is shot on the afternoon of one shoot day, inside weeks 6 to 8. Any
legible signage or other business names in frame are removed or kept out of focus.

## 4. How it will be used

- **The proof photograph** sits on our "How it works" page, beside the line "You've approved a
  proof before …". It is small, about 486 × 324 px on a desktop screen and 350 × 233 px on a phone,
  shot from overhead with a 50 mm macro lens, with a second frame at 30° on a 120 mm macro. The
  close frame shows the type, the impression and the paper's grain, so the printing has to be
  flawless up close. On the day, a pencil "OK" and a short pencil rule go in the margin of one
  copy; no initials and no signature.
- **On the same page**, our site draws the Harbor & Pine letterhead proof live on screen (crop
  marks, the centred letterhead) and turns it into the filing proof a firm approves. Your printed
  proof and our drawn one must look like the same piece.
- **The envelope** lies on a walnut desk, photographed from directly overhead on a medium-format
  camera: once with the address face up, papers edge-on beside it, and once turned flap-up so no
  address shows. That photograph is on "How it works" ("The papers come back to you."), at about
  708 × 531 px. Our home page also draws the same envelope in code, so the printed one must match
  it: heavy white wove, a fine edge, the name in Lora 600.

## 5. References and what to avoid

**References:** the work of a contemporary fine stationer: a proud firm's cards and letterhead,
printed beautifully, with real white space and precise rules.

**Avoid:**

- Anything of CoFile's on any piece: no mark, name, imprint or "printed by" line. These pieces
  belong to the firm.
- Recoloring, redrawing or retouching the firm's logo.
- Invented details: names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, websites, initials, signatures.
- Stamps, seals, embossed foil, wax, ribbons, or anything that looks official.
- Aged or antique effects: parchment, distressing, Victorian ornament, copperplate script.
- A postage stamp, postmark or window on the envelope.

2. **Ink drawdowns** of each ink on the chosen stock. We approve the match.

   else on it.
4. **The run.** Clean, unmarked copies and spares, packed flat so nothing creases.

**Accepted** means every check passes, the pieces reach the photographer's producer undamaged
before the shoot, and [CoFile contact] signs off.
