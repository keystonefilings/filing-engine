# Brief: reprographic scanning studio

**The job:** flat scans of cotton letter paper, heavy cotton card and black card at 1,200 dpi,
made into small seamless texture tiles for our website.
**For:** a reprographic studio that makes flat scans of art and paper (not on a flatbed).
**Contact:** [CoFile contact]

## 1. Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service. Accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms use it
to file state business filings for their clients (LLC formations, payroll registrations, EINs)
under the firm's own brand. We see ourselves as the firm's stationer, and our line for it is "Your
name on the door. Ours in small type." So our website is built like good stationery: warm white
cotton paper, dark ink, and for dark mode, black card printed in light ink.

## 2. What we need

Three papers, scanned flat at 1,200 dpi: a 100% cotton letter paper, a heavy cotton card, and a
black card. From the cotton paper and the black card we need seamless 512 px tiles. They lie
under our pages at a few percent strength, so the page feels like paper without anyone noticing a
pattern. The heavy card is scanned as a reference for the card our price list is drawn on and for
print pieces. Whether it also becomes a tile is still open.

   - **Black card**: weight and fibre not set in our spec; please advise.

4. **Tile 3, heavy cotton card**: as tiles 1 and 2 [owner to confirm whether this tile is made;

5. **The working files** for each tile: the seamless tile at full scan resolution before it is

6. **Option: a 2,400 dpi scan of a small brass plate** (120 × 48 mm, engraved "Co") that our

## 4. How it will be used

- **Cotton tile**: repeated across every page ground in our light look (our page color is Cotton
  `#FBFAF6`) at 3% strength, multiplied. That covers the website and the pages firms work in.
  Never on the white sheets that lie on the page: a client's details sit on clean white.
- **Black card tile**: repeated across our dark grounds (Night `#14161E`; the rehearsal band
  deepens to `#0E1016` in dark mode) at 4% strength, in screen mode. It appears on the one dark
  band of our home page (an empty stage under a ghost light) and across our pages in dark mode.
- **Heavy card**: the reference for how our price card's edge and shadow are drawn, and for print
  pieces. One page of our spec also lays this scan over the home page's price card at 2%; another
  says no texture goes on any sheet. That use is not settled [owner to confirm].
- **Scale and viewing:** full-screen backgrounds, from 390 px phones to 1,440 px desktops and
  beyond, often on high-density screens. At 3% and 4% the grain is barely there, so any seam,
  blotch or repeating fibre shows up as a pattern across a wide screen.
- The textures switch off for readers who ask for reduced transparency, on low-power devices and
  in high-contrast mode, so the page never depends on them.

## 5. References and what to avoid

**References:** real 100% cotton letter paper and real heavy card, as a fine stationer stocks them:
warm white, not cream.

**Avoid:**

- Cream, parchment or aged looks: no yellowing, stains, foxing, deckle-edge shadows or wear.
- Visible seams, or a fibre or speck that repeats noticeably when tiled.
- A color cast. The tiles are neutral grey; the page color is set in our code.
- Dust, hairs, scratches and scanner marks (cleaned out by hand, not blurred away).
- Added grain, sharpening halos or filters.
- Any printing, watermark or mill mark in the scanned area.

## 6. Rounds and acceptance

1. **Test scans and test tiles** of each paper at two or three scales. We check them on our own
   pages at their real strength (3% on Cotton, 4% on Night) on a desktop and a phone, and choose
   the scale.
2. **Final tiles and masters.** We check:
   - seamless, with no visible seam or obvious repeat across a full 1,440 px screen and wider;
   - 512 × 512 px, WebP, 40 KB or less each;
   - grey-balanced and cleaned;
   - masters at 1,200 dpi, lossless.

**Accepted** means every check passes, the tiles drop into our pages in place of today's generated
texture with no further work, and [CoFile contact] signs off.
