# Brief: hand engraver

**The job:** real brass and steel name plates for two example firms, a blank plate, a small brass
plate of our own mark, and test plates; a day at your bench on film; fitting the plates beside two
doors for our photographs.
**For:** a working bench engraver who still cuts name plates by hand with a burin.
**Contact:** [CoFile contact]

## 1. Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service. Accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms use it
to file state business filings for their clients (LLC formations, payroll registrations, EINs)
under the firm's own brand. We see ourselves as the firm's stationer, and our home page says so
beside two real office doors with two engraved plates: "Your name on the door. Ours in small type."

**About the names on the plates.** Harbor & Pine CPA and Northgate Payroll are fictional example
firms. They are not our clients and not real businesses; we use them in every picture to show what
CoFile does for a firm.

## 2. What we need

Every brass thing on our site is a photograph of a real object, so we need the real objects, made
by hand. Two plates for two fictional firms' doors (Harbor & Pine CPA in brass, Northgate Payroll in
brushed steel), the same brass plate left blank, a spare blank, a small brass plate carrying our
own mark's "Co" for our lettering artist to trace, and three brass test plates for the studio that
builds an engraving on screen. All are cut to the shape of our mark. We'd also like to film you
cutting the Harbor & Pine plate at your bench, and have you fit the plates beside the two doors on
the shoot days.

### 3.2 The shape (every plate)

Every plate is cut to our mark's 40 × 16 proportion:

- **Plates A, B, C and the spare:** 300 × 120 mm; all four corners chamfered at 45° with an
  18.75 mm leg; a 1.5 mm bevel round the face; two 4 mm slotted round-head screws on the centre
  line, 33.75 mm in from each end, their slots set **level**. The plate in every photograph is the
  plate in our logo.
- **A, B and the spare are drilled identically**, so the engraved plate can be swapped for the
  blank on the same screws in minutes (3.3).
- **The blank's face** must be evenly finished, with no scratch, mark or stamp, across the whole
  area between the screws (52.5 to 247.5 mm across, 26.25 to 93.75 mm down). On our site a
  visitor's own firm name is engraved there digitally, at a cap height of 14 mm, stepping down to
  9.8 mm and then to two lines at 7.7 mm.
- **Plate D**, our mark, at 120 mm wide (one grid unit = 3 mm): 120 × 48 mm; corners chamfered at
  45° with a 7.5 mm leg; two slotted screws centred on the centre line (24 mm down), 13.5 mm in from
  each end; the "Co" with a cap height of 19.2 mm, its baseline 33.6 mm below the top edge,
  optically centred, exactly as the lettering artist's drawing places it.
- **Screws:** steel on the steel plate. No other gold, copper or yellow metal may appear in our
  pictures besides the brass plates themselves; tell us what you'd use on the brass plates.

### 3.3 Days with us

The photographer's producer sets the exact days. In shoot order:

1. **Day 1, at your bench (the film).** You cut plate A on camera, start to finish. The film crew
   needs: the burin's point entering the brass, in extreme macro; the H cut, one curl of brass
   lifting off the graver; the italic ampersand in one sweep; a time-lapse of the whole name, the
   plate turning on your ball vise; black wax rubbed into the letters and wiped back. Also stills
   of you at the bench, the burin and the curl of brass, and the finished plate. Your hands are the
   stars; there is no speech.
2. **Day 2, at the Harbor & Pine door, noon to an hour after sunset.** You fit plate A on the brick

   photographed and then swapped for blank B on the same screws, so stay through the light.
3. **Day 3, at the Northgate door, mid-morning.** You fit plate C to the right of a glazed
   aluminium-framed door, its centre at 1,500 mm.
4. **Optional:** for longer 30- and 60-second versions of the film, cutting plate C on camera too

## 5. References and what to avoid

**References:** the plates a working engraver cuts for a good office door: plain, well-proportioned,
beautifully cut. Lettering drawn for each firm, not set in a stock engraving face.

**Avoid:**

- Machine or laser engraving. Every letter is cut by hand with a burin.
- Any border, ornament, crest or logo on the face besides the lettering; no maker's mark on the
  face (on the back is fine).
- Anything that looks like an award, a plaque of honour or an official seal.
- A mirror polish or gold plating: the face is satin-brushed and lacquered.
- Changing the drawings. The names are spelled and set exactly as drawn: the italic ampersand and
  small-capital "CPA" on A, capitals on C.

## 6. Rounds and acceptance

1. **Layouts and finish.** Full-size layouts of A, C and D on the plate outline, and the finish
   samples (lacquered brass, brushed steel). We check the measurements in 3.2, the lettering's
   placement and cap heights, and the brass against `#D9A84A`.
2. **Before the shoot.** B, C, D, the spare and the test plates finished and delivered; A's blank
   prepared with its layout transferred, ready to cut. We check: every measurement to the drawing;
   A, B and the spare drilled identically; faces clean and even; C's fill flush and black; lacquer
   even; no marks on the faces.
3. **On camera.** Plate A cut on day 1. We check the lettering against the drawing (italic
   ampersand, small-capital CPA, 14 mm cap height, centred between the screws) and the fill.
4. **On location.** We check: plates level, slots level, centres at 1,500 mm, the A-to-B swap done
   inside the light window.

**Accepted** means every check passes, the photographer's digital tech confirms on set that the
brass and steel render true on camera, and [CoFile contact] signs off.
