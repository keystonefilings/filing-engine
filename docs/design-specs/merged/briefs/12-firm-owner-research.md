# Firm-owner research: their brand on our pages, before the build is final

Weeks are counted from the start of our build (week 1).

## Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service for accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms.
Firms use it to file state business filings for their clients, such as LLC formations, payroll
registrations and EINs, under the firm's own brand. There are two sides to it:

- **The firm** works in CoFile's filing desk. The desk is in CoFile's look and is the same for every
  firm, with the firm's logo and name in its account label.
- **The firm's clients** see only pages in the firm's own name, logo, colors and font. Nothing of
  CoFile appears on those pages, apart from one line of legal fine print.

## What we need

We need a researcher to run moderated sessions with six to eight firm owners before our new design
is final. Each owner sees three things:

1. **The home page's pictures.** These are pictures of our product, built from the real product.
   They show a firm's desk with its client's page in front of it, the same client page in two
   firms' brands, a client's text message becoming a draft filing, the page a firm approves, the
   filed record coming home, and our coverage, rehearsal and price pictures.
2. **Their own client page,** in their own logo and colors.
3. **Our filing desk,** with their own logo and name in its account label.

Before each session we load the owner's logo and colors through our ink match. The ink match is our
system that turns any brand color into button, link and background shades that stay readable (at
least 4.5:1 contrast), without changing the hue. It also trims and sizes the logo. So each owner
sees what their clients would really see, with their real brand.

This can be the same consultancy that runs our accessibility audit, or a separate researcher.

## Scope

### What we want to learn

**The three main questions:**

1. **Does the firm feel it leads?** The home page says "Your name on the door. Ours in small type."
   Do the pictures and the words make the firm the one in charge, with CoFile behind it?
2. **Does the client page feel like theirs?** Consider the logo's size and placement, their colors as
   we handle them, the font, and the absence of any CoFile branding. Would they send it to a client
   today? Do they notice the one line of fine print that names CoFile? Does it matter to them?
3. **Does the desk read as theirs to use?** The desk is in CoFile's look, the same for every firm,
   with their logo and name in its account label. Does it feel like their own office, or like
   someone else's software? Would their staff work in it every day?

**Secondary questions, as time allows:**

- When the ink match deepens or adjusts their color, do they understand the one-line explanation,
  and do they accept the change?
- Is their logo handled well? Did the trimming, sizing and white or colored plate do it justice?
- On the desk, can they tell at a glance who sent each filing and where each one is up to? The desk
  shows this with a "Sent by" column and a five-step track.
- Is there anything that would stop them from offering the client page to their clients?

### Recruiting criteria

Six to eight participants, who should be:

- owners or partners who make the buying decision for their firm;
- from CPA, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms, with at least one of each type.
  [owner to confirm: the mix];
- from small and mid-sized practices, mostly aged 40 to 65;
- people who already file, or arrange, state filings for their clients (LLC formations, payroll
  registrations, EINs);
- firms with a logo and brand colors they can share;
- a mix of brands, ideally including:
  - a square symbol, a wide wordmark, and a light logo made for a dark website;
  - a dark color, a mid-tone color, and a light or red-family color.

**Exclude** people who work for filing, formation or registered-agent services.

### Before each session

1. The participant sends their logo file and brand colors, and the font they would pick from our ten
   preset fonts. They may send their own font file instead, if they have the right to share it.
2. We load these through our Branding page into a private test account, made for that participant
   only.
3. We check that the client page and the desk's account label look right, and note anything the ink
   match or the logo processing did.

### Session script outline

Please propose the timings. The order we suggest:

1. **Welcome and consent.** Explain the purpose and confirm consent for the recording and for the use
   of their logo. Remind them that we are testing the design, not them.
2. **Their practice today.** What filings they handle for clients, who on their staff does the work,
   and how their clients send them details now.
3. **The home page's pictures**, on a computer and then on a phone. Ask for first impressions:
   - Whose name do they notice first?
   - What does CoFile do, and who is in charge?

   Ask them to think aloud, and don't explain the pictures.
4. **Their own client page**, on their own phone, as their client would see it. Ask:
   - Is this yours?
   - What would you change?
   - Would you send it to a client tomorrow?

   Then point out the fine-print line only if they haven't noticed it. If the ink match adjusted
   their color, show the before and after and ask what they make of it.
5. **The desk, with their logo and name in its account label.** Ask them to find the filing that is
   waiting for them, say who sent it and where it is up to, and start a new filing. Then ask:
   - Whose office does this feel like?
   - Would your staff use it every day?
6. **Wrap-up.** Ask:
   - What three words describe it?
   - What would stop you using it?
   - Is anything missing?

## Standard or criteria

- The sessions are moderated one to one, and don't lead the participant. The researcher doesn't
  explain the design before the participant has reacted to it.
- Every participant sees their own brand. No session uses only our example firms.
- Findings separate what participants did from what they said.
- Each of the three main questions gets a clear answer for each participant, and an overall answer
  with evidence.

## Deliverables

1. **A findings report**, ranked by impact, that answers the three main questions for each
   participant and overall.
2. **Problems the ink match or the logo processing had with real brands:** for each, the color or
   logo, what happened, and what the participant thought of it.
3. **Recommended changes,** sorted into design changes, wording changes and changes to the ink match
   or logo processing.
4. **Notable quotes,** with no participant identified by name.
5. **Screenshots of each participant's client page and desk,** as loaded. These are for internal use
   only.
6. **Session recordings and notes,** delivered securely, for those who consented to recording.
7. **A read-out call** with the owner and the build team.
