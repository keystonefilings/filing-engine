## 1. Who we are

CoFile (cofile.app) is a filing service for accounting, payroll, fractional-CFO and title firms.
In the site's own words: "CoFile files your clients' formations, EINs and payroll registrations on
the official state and IRS websites, once you approve." Firms use it under their own brand, so their
clients see only the firm: "Your name on the door. Ours in small type."

## 2. The look

The idea behind the site is that CoFile is the firm's stationer: it engraves the firm's name on a
plate once, so everything the firm's clients see carries that name, beautifully. CoFile's own mark
is a name plate that is blank except for "Co": we make the plate, the firm puts its name on it. This
film is that idea, made real. A working hand engraver, the kind who still cuts name plates and
trophies by hand, cuts "Harbor & Pine CPA" into a brass plate at the bench, fills the letters with
black wax, and fits the plate beside the firm's harbor-blue door. It opens the site's page for
firms, beside the headline "Form the business. Keep the client."

It is craft documentary. The cutting is real, the tools are real, and the engraver's hands are the
stars. Nothing is added: no graphics, no titles, no sound.

**The same light and grade as the photographs.** Light from camera left: the bench's window and
practicals, then late sun on the door. The film is graded with the stills' LUT, so it sits with the
photographs on the page: neutral and slightly warm, whites at the site's paper white (Cotton
`#FBFAF6`), true blacks, skin as it is, and the brass reading as the brand's Brass `#D9A84A`. No
teal and orange, no haze, no vignetting, no added grain, no black and white. No other gold, copper
or yellow in frame than the plate.

**On the page it plays once and stops.** It starts, muted, the first time it is 40% in view, plays
for 12 seconds and holds its last frame: the plate fitted beside the door. It never loops. If it
scrolls off screen it pauses, and carries on from there when it returns. A replay control lets a
visitor watch it again.

**Never in frame:** legible real business names, signage or logos (the door's street is a real
street with its numbers and signs removed or out of frame); anything official (seals, stamps,
flags, emblems); anything branded on tools or clothing.

### 3c. The 12-second site cut, as the site plays it

- **Length:** 12 seconds.
- **Frame rate:** 24 fps. The macro shots are captured at 48 fps for a little slow motion; the
  time-lapse at one frame every two seconds.
- **Resolution:** captured at 6K; delivered at the sizes in 3b.
- **Aspect ratios:** 4:5 on desktop and tablet; 4:5 on phones from its own, tighter master; 16:9 for
  social.
- **No loop:** plays once and holds its last frame. Never loops.
- **Silence:** silent. No sound, no music, no speech, so no captions. It plays muted and inline.
- **Poster:** the last frame, the plate fitted beside the door, as AVIF. It loads first.
- **Reduced motion and data saving:** when a visitor has asked for reduced motion, or is saving
  data, or has no JavaScript, nothing autoplays. They see the poster (the last frame) with the play
  control. Print shows the poster too.
- **Text description:** a written description for screen readers, delivered as a WebVTT text
  description. CoFile writes the words (in short: an engraver cuts "Harbor & Pine CPA" into a brass
  plate and fits it beside the door); please give us a timed shot list so we can time it.
- **Web encodes:** AV1 and H.264 MP4, with no audio track, at the sizes in 3b. The page loads the
  film only once its section is within a screen, poster first. Size budget: question 2.

## 4. Casting and consent

- **The engraver.** The hand engraver who makes the plates, booked through their own brief. They
  appear in the film, and are paid as a craftsperson and credited by name under the film (CoFile
  writes the credit). Their hands are the stars. No actors and no hand doubles.
- **Nobody else** is in frame. At the door, passers-by stay out of frame.
- **The firm is fictional.** Harbor & Pine CPA is a fictional example firm, made to look like a
  believable modest practice and checked so it doesn't resemble any real firm. The cutting of its
  name is real.
- **Honesty.** By the owner's decision, the film carries no caption saying "example"; the
  engraver's credit is the only line under it. It is never presented as a customer's story.
- **Releases** (handled by the producer): a release from the engraver covering the film, the longer
  cuts and the stills; property releases for the engraver's workshop and for the door location
  (shared with the photographs). They cover web, email, advertising, social and print, worldwide,
  for five years and renewable. The engraver sees the cut before use, and can ask for it to be
  retired.

## 5. Props and dependencies

Made before the shoot by other vendors (briefed separately), or by the photo production:

- **The plates, from the hand engraver.** Every plate is 300 × 120 mm and 3 mm thick, cut to the
  CoFile mark's shape: four corners chamfered at 45° with an 18.75 mm leg, a 1.5 mm bevel round the
  face, and two 4 mm slotted round-head screws on the centre line, 33.75 mm in from each end.
  - **Plate A:** satin-brushed brass, lacquered against tarnish and fingerprints, cut on camera on
    day 1: "Harbor & Pine CPA" by hand with a burin, one line, cap height 14 mm, the ampersand
    italic, "CPA" in small capitals, centred between the screws, the letters filled with black wax
    and wiped flush.
  - **A spare blank** in brass, for a second take.
  - **Plate C, Northgate Payroll** (brushed 304 stainless steel, "NORTHGATE PAYROLL" in geometric
    capitals, cap height 12 mm), only if the longer cuts include it (question 5).
- **The lettering artwork**, the drawing of the firm's name the engraver cuts from: from CoFile.
- **The engraver's own tools:** burin, ball vise, black wax.
- **The door location (day 2):** found, cleared, painted if needed and released by the photo
  production. The engraver fits the plate there.
- **The grade:** question 7.

Related, on the photographer's list: on the door days the photographer records 1.2-second clips of
a reflector's light crossing each plate from the stills camera's lock-off. If the photographer's
