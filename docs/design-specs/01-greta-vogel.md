# Large Type

**A visual design spec for cofile.app**
Greta Vogel, Basel. Typography, grids, posters. Written for the build team and for the studios we commission.

The copy is locked and I have not touched a word of it. Every headline, label, price and caption quoted here comes from the repository (round 3, as published). Where a design needs words that do not exist yet (alt text, an accessible name for a control), I mark them `[copy needed]`.

---

## 1. Who I am, and what I bring

I trained in Basel in the late 1970s, when the school still taught that a poster is read from across the street in three seconds and then rewards you for standing closer. I have spent forty years on posters for theatres and museums, wayfinding for a regional transit network, and annual reports for banks and cultural foundations. My clients were institutions that needed to look serious without looking cold.

What I bring to CoFile is one conviction: **type can sell harder than any illustration.** This copy is short, declarative and confident: "Your name on the door. Ours in small type." A sentence like that does not need a drawing of a door. It needs to be set so that the reader feels the difference between large and small before they finish reading. That is my trade.

The home page now has about 330 words, and the brief says the pictures must carry it. A poster designer is used to that. A theatre poster has twelve words and one image, and it fills the house. My pictures will not be illustrations or screenshots: they will be typeset objects (sheets, slips, boards) composed with the same care as the headlines.

I also bring two habits from my wayfinding work. The first is legibility for people over fifty, under bad light, in a hurry. Most of the firm owners reading this site are my age or a little younger. The second is the timetable: how to make a long list of facts (sixteen filing types in four places, each at its own stage) feel calm and easy to scan.

I do not bring illustration, 3D or gradients. I will explain in each section why the site does not need them.

## 2. The visual concept: Large Type

### The idea in one sentence

**Your name in large type. Ours in small type. And every word a firm owner needs, large enough to read without glasses.**

The copy hands me the concept in its own words: "Your name on the door. Ours in small type." I take that sentence literally and make it the design system. The whole site is built on **two sizes in strong contrast**: one thing very large, and everything else at a generous reading size. Nothing sits in between to argue with them.

"Large type" means three things here, and each one sells part of the story.

1. **The firm's name is the largest name on every page.** On its filing desk, its client forms, its client link pages, its emails and the proof it approves, the firm's name and logo lead and CoFile is a 13-pixel grey "with CoFile". On CoFile's own home page every picture is shown in an example firm's brand, never in CoFile's, and the CoFile wordmark in the header is 22 px while the pitch beside it is set at 100 px and more. A firm owner should see a firm's name set beautifully and think: that is how we would look.
2. **The client's business name is set large wherever it appears.** On the desk's rows, in the drafted filing, at the top of the proof, on the papers that come back: "Two Sisters Bakery LLC" is the lead in five of the home page's pictures, the way a play has one lead. A firm's work is people and their businesses, not ticket numbers, and the design says so.
3. **The reading text is large.** Body text is 20 px on desktop and 18 px on a phone, with high contrast. This is a site for busy, proud, often older professionals. Large type is a courtesy, and it also reads as confidence: companies with something to hide use small type for everything, not just for their own name.

### The pictures are typeset objects

The owner has left the pictures to me: how many, where, and how they sit with the words. My home page has **ten pictures for nine sections**: seven product specimens that replace today's stand-ins (the desk, the same client page under two firms, a text becoming a draft, the proof, the papers, the state boards, the rehearsal programme), one typographic picture of my own (the price, set as the image of the pricing section), one black-and-white photograph (the firm's door, in the close), and the headline of section 2, which is set so that it becomes a picture itself. The specimens are designed as real product pages, built in live HTML and SVG with the example firm's real logo, colour and font, then composed like the objects on a poster.

- **One object, set large.** Each picture shows one thing (a sheet, a pair of pages, a board) big enough to read every word without zooming.
- **Cropped with intent.** Where a page is long, it runs off the edge of its field rather than being shrunk. A cropped sheet says "there is more, and it is real"; a shrunken one says "screenshot".
- **On a flat field.** White sheets on Poster Yellow, Ink, Ground or the firm's colour. No device frames, no browser chrome, no perspective, no shadows except one 1 px hairline where a white sheet meets white.
- **Always labelled.** Each keeps its small example caption ("Example · test details · not a real firm" and the like), set at 16 px under the picture, never hidden.
- **Live, not flat images.** Because they are HTML, they stay sharp on any screen, can move (section 8), can be read by screen readers and can be translated.

### The three rules of the system

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Two sizes** | Every composition has one large element (a headline, a name, a price, a numeral) and the rest at reading size. The display line is at least five times the body size. No decorative mid-size text. |
| **The name leads** | On the firm's pages, the largest name is the firm's or its client's, never CoFile's. On CoFile's own pages the CoFile wordmark is small while the pitch is set very large. |
| **One colour at a time** | Each field carries one colour. On CoFile's own stage that colour is Poster Yellow. On the firm's pages and in the pictures of them, it is the firm's colour. All photography is black and white, so a picture never competes with a firm's brand. |

### How the concept carries the selling story

| The copy sells | How Large Type shows it |
|---|---|

| **2. Your brand on everything.** | Section 2 sets "Your name on the door." in very large type and "Ours in small type." in, literally, small type on the same line. Beside it, two colour fields, pine and oxblood, each holding the same client page in a different firm's brand. |
| **3. Nobody retypes anything.** | Ana Ortiz's text message is set as large as a headline. The words that matter are marked with a yellow highlighter, and the same words reappear, marked the same way, in the drafted filing below. The saved answers are already sitting there. |
| **4. You stay in charge.** | The proof is a printer's proof: a clean sheet with crop marks, the firm's name at the top, every detail and fee in aligned figures, one button in the firm's colour. The firm owner signs it off the way they once signed off letterhead at the printer. |
| **5. It's done, and the papers come back.** | A filed record in the firm's brand with the state's document and the IRS letter laid on it, and the EIN set in large figures: the number a new business owner will quote for the rest of their life. |

### Why yellow

Poster Yellow is the colour of yes, and the hero says the word: "Say yes, under your own name." On CoFile's own pages yellow appears only around yes: the hero, where the firm says yes to its client; the highlighter on the details that came straight from the client's text (the work nobody had to retype); the headline on the rehearsal stage ("In beta: rehearse a filing, free."); the "You approve it." step on How it works; and the closing poster, "Run one rehearsal. Then decide." Black type on this yellow has a contrast of about 12 to 1, which is why transit systems and theatre posters have used it for a century. And almost no CPA or payroll firm uses yellow as its brand colour, so CoFile's stage never clashes with the firm standing on it. On the firm's pages there is no yellow at all.

### What the concept is not about

Large Type is about names, people and ease. The checks live on How it works and security is one line in the pricing section with a page of its own; both are set clearly and quietly, easy to find. There are no padlocks, shields, seals, stamps, ticks or vaults anywhere in this system. Where the copy says a fee "matches the proof", the design shows it the way a compositor would, by setting the two figures on the same vertical line, not with a badge. The motion never celebrates a stop.

## 3. Identity

The CoFile identity has three parts: the **wordmark**, the **Company sign** (a compact mark for small spaces) and the **"with CoFile"** lockup. Because CoFile's brand promise is to stay small on the firm's pages, the identity is built to work at 13 pixels first and at large sizes second.

### 3.1 The wordmark

**CoFile**, set as custom lettering based on Suisse Int'l Semibold (600), with a capital C and a capital F. No symbol is attached to it. It is the name, set very well.

Construction notes for the lettering artist (all measurements in units of the cap height, H):

- **Base:** Suisse Int'l Semibold. Keep the typeface's x-height and stroke weights; this is spacing and detail work, not a new design.
- **Spacing:** overall tracking −1%. Tighten the C–o pair so the o's left curve sits 0.06 H from the C's upper terminal. Keep o–F at the font's spacing. Close F–i so the i's stem sits 0.09 H from the end of the F's top arm.
- **The i's dot:** square, 0.16 H on a side, top edge aligned with the cap height. It is the wordmark's only drawn detail, and it is the reason the wordmark stays crisp at 13 px, where a round dot turns into a smudge.
- **Terminals:** the C and e terminals are cut horizontally, as in the base font.
- **The l:** ascends to cap height plus 0.04 H, so the word has a quiet rhythm of two tall forms (C, F) and one slightly taller (l).
- **Colour:** Ink (#121212) on Paper or on Poster Yellow; Paper (#FFFFFF) on Ink. Never yellow on white, never in a firm's colour, never in a gradient, never outlined.
- **Clear space:** 1 H on all sides.
- **Minimum size:** cap height 9 px on screen (about a 13 px font size); 3 mm cap height in print.
- **Header size:** 22 px font size on desktop, 20 px on phones. On a page whose pitch is set at 132 px, a 22 px wordmark makes the point without a word.

**Do not:** stretch it, set it in capitals ("COFILE"), split it ("Co File"), put it in a box, give it a tagline lockup, or make a giant wordmark across the footer. That fashionable device would contradict the copy.

### 3.2 The mark: the Company sign, Cº

For two hundred years, firms wrote their partners into their names as "& Cº": a capital C followed by a small raised o with a short line under it. It was painted on shopfronts, engraved on brass door plates, printed on letterheads and invoices. It is the "and Company" after the founders' names, in small print.

CoFile is that partner: the Co. that comes after your firm's name. So the mark is a **Cº**, drawn new: a capital C with a superior o and a bar beneath it. It also enacts the concept in one glyph, a large letter and a small one. It is a genuine piece of commercial typography from the world of firms, door plates and ledgers, and it belongs to no one's brand.

Construction, on a 100 × 100 unit square (y measured from the top):

| Part | Geometry |
|---|---|
| **C** | Drawn from the wordmark's C. Occupies x 6–64, y 14–86 (72 units tall, plus 1 unit overshoot top and bottom). Vertical stroke 13 units, top and bottom strokes 11.5 units. Terminals cut horizontally. The upper terminal ends at x 62; the lower at x 63. The opening on the right is 26 units tall. |
| **Superior o** | A true circle, not an ellipse. Outer diameter 30 units, stroke 8.5 units (relatively heavier than the C, so it holds at small sizes). Occupies x 65–95, y 14–44: its top is aligned with the top of the C, and it nests beside the C's upper terminal with a 3-unit gap. |
| **Bar** | A rectangle x 65–95, y 50–57: exactly as wide as the o, 7 units tall, 6 units below the o. Its top edge lies on the C's horizontal centre line. The bar is what makes the sign read as "Company" and not as a degree sign. |
| **Overall** | The mark sits optically centred in its square: shift the whole group 1 unit left. Bounding box x 5–94, y 13–87. |

Colour rules:

- On Paper: everything in Ink.
- On Ink: C and o in Paper, the bar in Poster Yellow. This is the only place the mark carries two colours.
- On Poster Yellow: everything in Ink.
- **App icon / touch icon:** a Poster Yellow tile (platform corner radius), the mark in Ink at 64% of the tile width.
- **Favicon:** hand-hinted versions at 16, 32 and 48 px. At 16 px: C 11 px tall with a 2 px stroke, o 5 px with a 1.5 px stroke rendered at 2 px, bar 5 × 2 px. Draw these pixel by pixel; do not let the browser scale the vector down.

Where the mark appears: the favicon and app icon; the avatar on CoFile's own emails; the 404 page; the footer of CoFile's own pages, where it sits before the line "CoFile · the filing desk behind your firm". **Where it never appears:** on any page, email or widget that belongs to a firm. There, CoFile is only the words "with CoFile".

### 3.3 The "with CoFile" mark: ours in small type

The copy says it twice: "Ours in small type", and on For firms, "The only sign of us is a small 'with CoFile'." So this is a line of small type, not a badge.

- **Set as:** "with" in Suisse Int'l Regular, then a word space of 0.28 em, then "CoFile" in the wordmark lettering at the same cap height.
- **Size:** 13 px on the web (desktop and phone), 12 px in emails. Never larger than 15 px, and never more than a quarter of the firm logo's height.
- **Colour:** Graphite #54544F on white (7.6:1 contrast, so it is small but perfectly readable). On a dark desk header in dark mode: #A9A9A3. Never in a colour, never in the firm's colour, never with the Cº mark, never in a pill or box.
- **Position:** at the right end of the firm's header bar, vertically centred on the logo. On phones narrower than 340 px it drops below the header as a right-aligned line. In the embed widget the existing copy "Filed with CoFile" sits at the bottom left of the form at 12 px. On the proof it sits at the top right of the brand bar. In client emails it is in the footer.
- **Link:** on the client's pages and in client emails, it is not a link. A client should never be pulled away from their firm. On the firm's own filing desk it may link to CoFile's help address.
- **White-label (platforms):** no "with CoFile" at all, as the copy promises ("with your name and support contact on the proof").

### 3.4 The name plate: how a firm's logo sits on its pages

Every firm page starts with a name plate: a white header bar, the firm's logo or name on the left, "with CoFile" on the right, and a 4 px rule in the firm's main colour running the full width of the window beneath it. It is a brass plate beside an office door, translated to a screen: white, calm, with the name standing on it.

**The bar:** pure white (#FFFFFF) in every mode, because most firm logos are JPEGs with white backgrounds and any tinted ground would show a box around them. Height 72 px desktop, 64 px phone. A 4 px rule in the firm's main colour runs under it, edge to edge.

**Logo rules, applied automatically at upload from the image's aspect ratio** (width ÷ height):

| Logo shape | How it is placed |
|---|---|
| **Wide wordmark** (ratio 2.5 or more), for example Northgate Payroll's or Best Accounting's | Logo alone, 36 px tall on desktop, 32 px on phone, maximum 220 px wide (180 px on phone). The firm's name is kept as hidden text for screen readers, not repeated on screen. |
| **Medium** (1.6 to 2.5) | Logo 44 px tall (36 px phone), maximum 180 px wide. Name hidden as above. The branding page preview lets the firm see the result. |
| **Symbol or square** (under 1.6), for example Harbor & Pine's pine-and-wave symbol | Logo 44 px tall (36 px phone), then 12 px of space, then the firm's name set in the firm's font at 20 px (18 px phone), weight 600 if the font has it, else 500, in Ink. |
| **No logo** | The firm's name is typeset as a name plate (rules below). |

All logos keep clear space of half their height on each side. A logo is never recoloured, outlined, placed in a circle or given a shadow.

**Typesetting a firm's name when there is no logo.** This is where a typographer can make a firm look good for free:

- The name is set in the firm's chosen font at 22 px (20 px phone), in Ink, with tracking adjusted by font class: −0.01 em for sans serifs, 0 for serifs, +0.02 em for monospaced or condensed faces.
- **The ampersand:** if the font family includes an italic, the "&" is set in the italic. "Harbor *&* Pine CPA". It is an old compositor's courtesy and it makes almost any name look considered.
- **Credentials and suffixes** ("CPA", "LLC", "& Co.", "PLLC"): set in the font's small capitals when it has them; otherwise at 88% size with +0.04 em tracking, so they don't shout.
- The name never wraps in the header. If it is too long for the space, it drops to 18 px; if it still does not fit, it truncates on the filing desk only (never on client pages, where it moves to a second line under the bar).

**The firm's colour, on its pages:**

- **Main colour:** the 4 px rule under the name plate, the primary button fill and the current-page underline in the desk navigation. The setup form already tells firms "Button text is white, so choose a dark color." The system checks it: if white text on the main colour falls below 4.5:1, buttons use a darkened version of the same hue (darkened in steps of 4% lightness, in OKLCH, until it passes). The rule keeps the true colour.
- **Second colour:** links and the selected-row tint (the second colour at 8% over white). If it falls below 4.5:1 on white for link text, links fall back to Ink with an underline in the second colour.
- **Nothing else.** No firm-colour backgrounds behind text, no gradients made from the firm's colour, no CoFile yellow anywhere near it.

**The firm's font, on its pages:** the firm's font is used for the firm's name, the page's main heading (h1) and section headings. Body text, labels, fields, tables and fee figures stay in Suisse Int'l, so a firm that picks a script or a display face still gets forms a client can read at arm's length. If a firm uploads a font, it is loaded with `font-display: swap` and size-adjusted (`size-adjust`, `ascent-override`) to match Suisse's x-height, so the page doesn't jump when it arrives.

On a dark screen, a full field of yellow glares. So in dark mode the yellow fields turn into dark fields with yellow display type, like a theatre poster at night. The firm's colour fields stay as they are, because the firm's main colour is dark by rule.

**What stays light in dark mode:** the proof, always. It is a sheet of paper, and the firm approving a filing should see it exactly as it will be printed and kept. The proof sits on the dark page as a white sheet. The firm's client-facing pages (intake form, service pages, client links, embed) also stay light, because the firm's logo and colours were chosen for white paper. The filing desk (the firm staff's own working pages) follows the system setting, with the name plate bar kept white so the logo is never on dark.

### 4.3 How colour is used

- **One colour per field.** A section is white, yellow, black or the firm's colour. Never two colours side by side in one field, never a gradient, never a tint of yellow as a background.
- **Rough proportions on the home page:** 60% Paper, 18% Poster Yellow, 14% Ink, 8% firm colour (the twin fields of section 2).
- **Text is always Ink or Paper.** Colour is for fields, rules, the highlighter and one button. No coloured headlines on white.
- **Photography is black and white.** It never introduces a second colour.
- **On the firm's pages,** the palette is Paper, Ink, Graphite, Hairline and the firm's two colours. CoFile contributes no colour at all.
- **The highlighter** is a band of Poster Yellow behind Ink text, 0.92 em tall and offset 0.08 em below the text's centre, so it looks like a felt-tip pass and not like a selection box. It marks only the words that came from a client's text and the places they land in the draft, in CoFile's own marketing pictures. On a firm's real proof there is no highlighter: every value is equal.

## 5. Typography

### 5.1 Typefaces

**Suisse Int'l** (Swiss Typefaces), for everything: display, text, interface. Weights Regular 400, Medium 500 and Semibold 600, with italics for Regular and Medium.
**Suisse Int'l Mono**, Regular 400 and Medium 500, for code and machine strings only.

Why one family: in Basel we were taught that two grotesques on one page is a mistake you make once. A single, very good neo-grotesque with a large x-height is clear at 13 px and magnificent at 132 px. Suisse has the open apertures, the generous x-height and the even colour that older readers need on screens, and it sets Spanish accents cleanly at display sizes. It is neutral enough that a firm's own font, standing beside it, looks like the guest of honour.

- **Licence:** web licence covering cofile.app, every firm page under it (including `cofile.app/<firm>/<service>` pages, client links and the proof) and the embed widget on firms' own sites. Desktop licence for the studio; app licence if a native app ever comes.
- **Files:** WOFF2, subset to Latin plus Latin Extended-A (Spanish á é í ó ú ü ñ ¿ ¡, and names such as "Lucía", "Muñoz", "Gonçalves"), with `tnum`, `lnum`, `case` and `kern` features kept.
- **Fallback stack:** `"Suisse Int'l", "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`. Mono: `"Suisse Int'l Mono", "SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace`.
- **Emails:** Helvetica / Arial (web fonts are unreliable in mail clients). The layout is designed so the system holds with Arial.
- **Commissioned:** the wordmark lettering and the Cº mark (section 3), from a type designer who works in this genre. Optional, in a second year: a custom companion cut of the face with CoFile's square i-dot and a heavier tabular figure set for fee tables.

### 5.2 The scale

A scale of about 1.3 (close to a perfect fourth), with two jumps where the poster begins. All sizes are fluid between the phone and desktop values (CSS `clamp()`), and all line heights snap to a 4 px grid.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `display-1` | 118 px (max 132 at 1680+) | 84 px | 46 px | 0.95 | −0.025 em | Medium | The hero headline; "Your name on the door."; the close. |
| `display-2` | 84 px | 64 px | 38 px | 1.0 | −0.02 em | Medium | Headlines of the home page's sections; the h1 of each inner page. |

### 5.3 How the type is set

- **Flush left, ragged right, always.** Nothing is centred and nothing is justified. Numbers in tables are right-aligned on the decimal.
- **Headline breaks are set by hand** on desktop for the poster headlines (the hero, firms, pricing and close, and the h1 of each inner page; breaks are given in sections 9 and 10), using breaks that apply at 1024 px and wider only. Everywhere else headlines use `text-wrap: balance` and paragraphs `text-wrap: pretty`.
- **Measure:** body text at most 64 characters per line; ledes at most 34 em.
- **Weight carries emphasis.** Medium for leads and labels, Semibold only for totals and the firm's typeset name. No Light or Thin anywhere: they vanish for older eyes.
- **Sentence case everywhere.** No letter-spaced capitals for kickers or labels. The kicker "For CPA, payroll, fractional CFO and title firms" is set in Medium at body size, not as a tiny capitalised label.
- **Capitals only where they are data:** "TWO SISTERS BAKERY LLC" is in capitals because that is how the state receives it. Set with the `case` feature on so punctuation sits at capital height.
- **Figures:** tabular lining figures (`tnum`) in every table, every fee and every date on the proof, so $100.00, $3.50 and $103.50 align on the point. Proportional figures in running text.
- **Glyphs:** typographer's apostrophes and quotation marks (’ “ ”) and real en dashes in ranges. The words stay exactly as written; only the glyph changes. (Build note: render the copy's `&apos;` as U+2019. Confirm with the copy owner; it changes characters, not words.)
- **Hanging punctuation:** opening quotation marks, the "$" of a price set at display size and list rules hang into the margin, so the text's left edge stays optically straight.
- **Links:** underlined, 1.5 px, offset 0.18 em, in the text colour. Hover: the underline thickens to 3 px. Links are never colour-only.
- **Mono is for machines.** Code (`"dryRun": true`), API keys, filing references, page URLs and widget snippets. Never for a client's name, address or fee. A bakery's name is not code.

### 5.4 Pairing with a firm's font

On the firm's pages, the firm's font takes the firm's name, the h1 and section headings; Suisse Int'l takes everything else. To make any font sit well:

- The firm font is size-matched to Suisse's x-height (CSS `size-adjust`) so a serif with a small x-height doesn't look timid next to the sans body.
- Headings in the firm font use weight 600 if available, else 500, else 400; tracking by class as in section 3.4.
- If the firm font is a script or decorative face (detected from the Google Fonts category, or flagged in setup review), it is used for the firm's name only and the headings fall back to Suisse.
- The proof's h1 (the client's business name) is set in the firm's font. It is the one place where the firm's typeface and the client's name meet in large type.

### 6.4 How pages breathe

- **Desktop:** generous but not empty. A reading section is usually 720–1100 px tall. The hero is at least 100% of the window height, capped at 960 px, and the desk sheet in it crosses the bottom edge of the yellow field by 160 px into the next section. - **Phone:** one column, 20 px margins. Fields and photographs run edge to edge so a phone still gets the poster. Headlines stay flush left at `display-*` phone sizes, which are still big: 46 px for the hero. Lists indent their text 24 px with the en rule hanging in that space. Nothing sits side by side on a phone except a label and a figure in a table row.
- **Tablet:** the eight-column grid takes the desktop layouts with asides moving under their text (columns 1–8) where the desktop puts them in 8–12.
- **No scroll tricks.** No sections pinned to the screen while content slides past, no parallax, no snap. A reader of sixty scrolls at their own pace.

### 7.4 Photography

**The look.** Black and white, documentary, available light. Deep, clean blacks and open whites, a fine grain (shot on a high-resolution digital back and converted, or on 400-speed film for the portraits). The light is the light of an ordinary weekday morning in an office or a shop: window light, practical lamps, nothing staged with strobes. People are photographed doing their work, not looking at the camera and laughing.

**Why black and white.** Every page of this site is someone's brand: a firm's pine green, a payroll company's oxblood. A colour photograph brings a third colour (a red sweater, a blue sky) and fights them. Black and white sits under any brand like a good grey suit. It also gives a 62-year-old CPA a site that looks like a well-made annual report rather than a startup.

**Cropping.** Hard and intentional, as on a poster. A face may be cut at the forehead; a hand may enter from the edge; the subject sits off-centre with real space for the type. Photographs are placed edge to edge or on exact column edges, never with rounded corners, frames or shadows, and never with type set over a face. Type set over a photograph is allowed only on a flat, empty area of it (a wall, a door panel), and only in Ink or Paper with at least 7:1 contrast measured on that area.

**Casting and locations.** Real firm owners and staff (CPA, payroll, title and fractional CFO firms) and real small-business owners in New Jersey, New York and Florida, paid and released. Ages from 30 to 70, and the mix of people you meet in Jersey City, Queens and Miami, including Spanish-speaking owners. Real offices with real clutter, cleaned of anything branded. - **Texture.** No paper grain, noise or gradients on the screen. A pixel that pretends to be paper is a lie told to an accountant. Real paper appears only in photographs.

## 8. Motion

### 8.1 Principles

Motion on this site behaves like a compositor placing pieces on a forme: each piece arrives once, lands exactly on the grid and stays still. It is used to show **work being done for the firm** (a text becoming a draft, papers coming back, a rehearsal running), never to decorate, and never to dramatise a stop or a check.

- Each moment plays **once**, when its picture is at least 40% in view, and is remembered for the session so it does not replay on scrolling back.
- Nothing loops. Nothing moves while the reader is reading text beside it, except the moment they just scrolled to.
- Type in headlines never animates. A headline is readable in the first frame.
- Prices, fees and figures never count up.
- No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no pinned sections, no springs or bounces.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, every moment shows its final state at once; only 100 ms opacity fades remain.

### 8.2 Tokens

### 8.4 Interface motion

- **Buttons:** on press, the button moves 1 px down and its fill darkens 6% (`t-press`). No ripples.
- **Focus:** appears instantly. Focus is never animated.
- **Accordions** (Questions, "Progress on the state site"): height and a chevron rotation in `t-ui`.
- **Notices** that appear after an action slide 8 px down and fade in `t-ui`; they stay until dismissed or the page changes.
- **Page changes:** none. A new page simply appears.
- **The proof, after the firm decides:** the decision page replaces it with a 180 ms fade. No celebration, no confetti, no stamp.

### 8.5 Building it

CSS transitions and the Web Animations API, triggered by `IntersectionObserver`. No animation library and no video: every moment is the real HTML of its picture, so it is sharp, accessible and light. Each moment's final state is the page's default markup; motion is added only when the script runs and reduced motion is off, so a reader without JavaScript sees every picture complete.

## 12. Craft notes

**Dark mode.** CoFile's pages and the filing desk follow the system setting (4.2). Yellow fields turn dark with yellow headlines; Ink fields go to pure black with a hairline edge. Specimens keep their white sheets, because they are pictures of paper. Black-and-white photographs are not inverted or dimmed. The proof, the firm's client pages and the widget are always light. Test every firm colour on `#141413` too: where the desk uses the firm's colour as text or a square on dark, it is lightened in OKLCH until it reaches 4.5:1.

**Phones.** Design at 390 px first and check at 360 and 430. Pictures run edge to edge; text has 20 px margins. Primary actions are full width and 56 px tall; the public site keeps "Run a free rehearsal" in a bar at the bottom of the screen after the hero. Nothing depends on hover. No horizontal page scroll at any width down to 320 px; tables and code scroll inside themselves. Forms use the right keyboards (`inputmode`, `autocomplete`) and never zoom on focus (inputs at 19 px).

**Older eyes.** Body text 18 to 20 px, never below 16 px except "with CoFile". Every line of text 7:1 or better, except display type of 56 px and more, which is at least 4.5:1 (on the firm's colour fields). Weights Regular and above only. Links always underlined. No type over busy photographs. Meaning never by colour alone: squares come in three shapes and every status has its words. The pages reflow to one column at 200% zoom. With `prefers-contrast: more`, Hairlines become Graphite and Ground panels gain a 1 px Graphite edge.

**Spanish text length.** Plan for 25 to 30% more length. The hand-set headline breaks are for English; with `lang="es"` they switch to `text-wrap: balance`, and display sizes step down one level when a word would overflow its column (container queries, not viewport guesses). Buttons wrap to two lines rather than truncate. Nothing has a fixed width that holds text. The two-size headline of section 2 keeps its second sentence small in every language. Hyphenation `auto` for Spanish body text, never in headlines. "¿" and "¡" hang into the margin like opening quotes. Test every specimen with Spanish strings and names with accents ("Lucía Ortiz Muñoz").

## 14. What I'd strip from today's visuals, and why

| Today | Why it goes | In its place |
|---|---|---|
| **The manila folder-tab mark with a check cut into it** | A check is a seal of approval, and a folder tab is the oldest cliché of document software. It also puts CoFile's brand on stopping and approving, which the brief rules out. | The Cº, the "and Company" of firms' names, drawn small. |
| **The warm cream ground** (the `#F7F3EA` paper on the proof) | Every white-background JPEG logo shows as a box on it, and it makes the product look antique. | Pure white paper. Warmth comes from yellow, people and type. |
| **Three typefaces** (Source Serif 4 headlines, IBM Plex Sans text, IBM Plex Mono values) | Three voices on one page. The serif headlines whisper where the copy speaks plainly, and mono values make a bakery's name look like code. | One grotesque for everything, and mono only for machines. |
| **A green tick before every list item** | Ticks turn every benefit into a box someone checked. Down a whole page, it reads as a compliance checklist, and it builds the look around verifying. | En rules, as a book would set them. |
| **Numbered callout circles on the sample proof** | Circles are the language of instruction manuals. | Marginal numerals, as in an annotated edition. |
| **"Matches the proof" as a green badge, and "held, on purpose" in its own warning tone** | They make the checks and the stop the loudest moments of their pictures. | Alignment of figures for the match; the held line set like every other line. |
| **Coloured dots as the only sign of a filing's stage** | Three dots in three colours are hard to tell apart for older and colour-blind eyes. | Squares in three shapes (filled green, filled ink, hollow), always with the words available. |
| **Soft rounded buttons and bordered cards everywhere** | Generic, and a bordered card around everything is a page with no hierarchy. | Square, flat rules and fields; three kinds of panel only. |
| **Small, muted grey type for lines that sell** (such as "Built and run by filing professionals…") | Muted small type says "fine print". These lines are promises. | Full-size Ink type. Small type is reserved for one phrase: "with CoFile". |
| **The same text-beside-picture layout in every section** | Nine identical spreads in a row make a page that is easy to scroll past. | Alternating sides, full-bleed fields and one poster rhythm: yellow, white, firm colour, black, yellow. |
| **The "H&P" letters in a box as the example firm's logo** | A placeholder logo makes the example firm, and so the reader's firm, look like a placeholder. | A designed kit for each example firm, so the system is shown at its best. |
